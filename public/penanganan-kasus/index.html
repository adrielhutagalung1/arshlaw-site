How we handle your case

What happens from the moment you first contact us until your matter is resolved, and what you should prepare.

The stages

Not every matter goes through every stage. Many are resolved before they reach a court.

  1. 01

    First conversation

    Tell us briefly about your matter by WhatsApp or email. We ask about the key points and the documents we need to see, and check for any conflict of interest before going further.

  2. 02

    Assessment and written proposal

    We review your documents, explain your legal position, the options open to you and their risks, and give you a written proposal before any work begins.

  3. 03

    Engagement

    Once you accept the proposal, you sign a power of attorney and we begin acting on your behalf.

  4. 04

    Out of court first

    Where possible, we start with a demand letter, negotiation, or mediation. This route often saves time and cost.

  5. 05

    In court

    When a matter has to go before a court, we prepare and file the case documents, including through e-Court, and attend the hearings.

  6. 06

    Deadlines and updates

    Every deadline is recorded from the start, from pleadings and evidence to appeals and their briefs. You receive regular updates, and a report whenever a judgment or decree is issued.

  7. 07

    After the judgment

    We explain the judgment, the remedies available and their deadlines, and how it can be enforced. Whether to continue or stop remains your decision.

What to prepare

You do not need everything ready before you contact us, but these documents help us understand your matter faster.

  • A short chronology: what happened, when, and who was involved.
  • The agreement, decision letter, or other documents at the centre of the matter.
  • Related correspondence and messages, including any demand letters sent or received.
  • Any judgment, decree, or court summons you have received, with the date you received it.

Watch the dates. Appeals are bound by short deadlines. If you have just received a judgment or decree, contact us promptly so there is still enough time left.

Confidentiality. What you tell us is kept confidential. When we write about a case for educational purposes, we refer to it by its case number and initials, without the full names of the parties.
